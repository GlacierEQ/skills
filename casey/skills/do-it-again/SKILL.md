# Do It Again

## Trigger

Use when the operator says `do it again`, requests another refinement cycle, or invokes recursive improvement on an existing artifact or system.

## Objective

Produce a materially superior canonical version. Do not merely paraphrase, lengthen, decorate, or restart.

## Required Inputs

- current canonical artifact;
- prior version or iteration history when available;
- operator objective;
- new evidence, constraints, or feedback;
- unresolved defects and open dependencies.

## Procedure

1. Freeze the strongest validated components.
2. Identify defects: omissions, contradictions, ambiguity, unsupported claims, structural weakness, duplication, unsafe assumptions, and integration gaps.
3. Rank defects by impact.
4. Repair the highest-impact defects first.
5. Preserve provenance, identifiers, citations, and version history.
6. Produce the improved canonical artifact.
7. Emit a delta record describing retained, added, changed, deprecated, and unresolved elements.
8. Test the artifact against its purpose and exit criteria.
9. When the current leg is stable and further iteration has diminishing value, declare the exit criteria met and advance to the next logical leg.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Never invent facts, sources, citations, deadlines, docket history, or tool results.
- Never discard validated intelligence without recording why.
- Never create a disconnected replacement when the canonical artifact can be evolved.
- Do not remain trapped in endless polishing.
- Do not advance while a critical defect remains unresolved unless the defect is explicitly carried forward as a dependency or risk.

## Required Output

- canonical version;
- version identifier;
- material delta;
- unresolved risks;
- exit-criteria status;
- next-leg handoff when advancement is justified.

## Completion Test

The iteration is complete only when it is materially stronger, traceable to the prior version, and either ready for another justified cycle or formally handed to the next leg.
